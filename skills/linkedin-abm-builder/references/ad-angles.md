# Ad angles for 1:1 ad sets

The detail behind Method step 7 in `SKILL.md`. Every example below is made up.

## The 3 default angles

Each ad answers a different question the buying group might have. Same audience, same week, 3 reasons to care.

### A. Their moment

Why now, for this account. Built from a public trigger in `trigger_note`.

- Good triggers: a new site or market, a new finance or IT leader, a system migration, a hiring wave for the team you help, a published growth goal.
- Formula: `<the trigger> means <the problem it creates>. <What you make easier>.`
- Example: "2 new distribution centers means 2 new stacks of supplier invoices. Keep every site on 1 approval queue."

### B. Peer proof

Someone like them already did it. 1 customer, 1 number.

- Match the customer to the account on the dimension the buyer cares about most: industry first, then size, then the trigger.
- Formula: `<Customer like them> <did the thing> and <moved 1 number>. <What stayed the same>.`
- Example: "A 6-warehouse food distributor cut invoice approval time from 9 days to 3. Same suppliers, same ERP."
- Use only proof the customer has approved for advertising.

### C. The offer

What they get if they click, made specific to them.

- Good offers: a demo on their own process, a teardown or audit, a benchmark against peers, a working session with a specialist.
- Formula: `See <your product> on <their process> in <time>, with <a concrete takeaway>.`
- Example: "See it run on your own approval rules in a 20-minute demo, with a map of where invoices stall today."
- Match the call to action button to the offer: Request demo for a demo, Download for a report, Register for an event, Learn more otherwise.

## Alternates when a default doesn't fit

| Angle | Use when | Example opener |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role split | The buying group has 2 groups with different pains, such as finance and IT | "Your IT team sets up 1 integration and is done." |
| Cost of waiting | The account has a public deadline, such as a migration date | "Moving ERPs next quarter? Set up invoice automation before cutover." |
| Switching | The account publicly uses a tool you replace | Talk about the job the tool does. Naming a competitor in an ad brings policy and legal review. |
| Event | You will meet them at a conference or local event | "Meet us at booth 214 in Chicago." |

## Writing rules

- **The swap test.** Swap the headlines of 2 ads. If both still make sense, they share an angle; rewrite 1.
- **Hook within 150 characters, headline within 70.** LinkedIn's single image specs say longer text gets truncated.
- **1 idea per ad.** A trigger, a proof point or an offer, never all 3.
- **Plain language.** Write the way the buyer's team talks in meetings. Spell out terms in the first ad a person sees.
- **Name the account sparingly.** In a 1:1 ad set, naming the company in angles A and C is common. Check the brand's rules first.
- **Don't use the account's logo** in the image unless your legal team has cleared it (default).

## Never use

These read as surveillance and can end a deal:

- Website visits, intent data, ad engagement or anything from your CRM ("you looked at pricing last week", "you asked for a demo last year").
- A lost deal, a churned contract or a support ticket.
- Layoffs, lawsuits, disputes, outages, executive departures, bad press.
- Personal details about a named person.

If the only trigger for an account is private, angle A uses a public industry angle instead, and the brief says so.

## Clusters

- Write to the shared situation, never to 1 company.
- Peer proof should match the whole cluster, such as the same industry.
- The offer can mention company size or industry ("built for small finance teams").

## Image brief

1 line per ad: the subject, the 1 number or phrase on the image, and the format. For example: "Product screenshot of the approval queue, overlay '1 queue, every site', 1200 x 628 landscape." LinkedIn's single image specs list 1200 x 628 landscape, 1200 x 1200 square and 720 x 900 vertical as recommended sizes.
